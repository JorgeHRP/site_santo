SantoScala Mora

From the Simón Bolívar Orchestra to the stages of Vienna and Graz.

Third generation in a family of musicians. Trained at UNEARTE and the Kunstuniversität Graz, he moves between the symphony orchestra, European chamber music and Venezuelan roots repertoire, and teaches where music still doesn't reach.

Origin
The house he was born into had one simple rule: each child would follow an art. His arrived through his father's hands: a cellist, conductor and luthier in Trujillo.

The legacy began with his grandfather, an Italian clarinetist and tailor who emigrated to Venezuela after the Second World War. Santo Scala Bastidas, his father and first teacher, instilled technical discipline from childhood. By age 15, Santo was already principal cellist of his home state's orchestra, and was giving his first lessons that same year.

The grandfather

Italian clarinetist and tailor, who emigrated to Venezuela in the postwar years.

The father

Santo Scala Bastidas: cellist, conductor and luthier in Trujillo. His first teacher.

The teachers

Valmore Nieves and Edgar Calderón, principal cellists of the two Simón Bolívar orchestras; later, Julian Arp in Graz.

The Four Strings

Four callings tuned to the same instrument.

Tap a string to open it. Each one carries a part of the work, and none plays alone.

Principal cellist at 15 in the Trujillo orchestra. Between 2017 and 2020, he was a member of the Simón Bolívar Symphony Orchestra of Venezuela, under the baton of Gustavo Dudamel and Diego Matheus, and led the cello section in an opera gala with the country's leading singers. In Brazil, he played in the Youth Orchestra of the State of São Paulo and held a scholarship at the Music Academy of the São Paulo State Symphony Orchestra (Osesp). In 2023, he served as principal cellist on a 15-concert tour across China. In Graz, he took the principal cello chair of the university orchestra, performed at the Musikverein Graz and collaborated with the Klagenfurt Opera orchestra. In 2026, he took the first cello desk of the Camerata Strumentale Siciliana for the concert season.

Trained in the chamber rigor of Alexander Pavlovsky, Chia Chou and Julian Arp, he made his debut at the Musikverein Graz alongside his teacher and university faculty in the institution's most important annual concert. Santo Scala made his debut at the prestigious Fränkischer Sommer Festival in Germany, performing with the Ilumina collective and sharing the stage with world-renowned figures such as Liza Ferschtman and Jennifer Stumm. In Austria, he collaborates with the acclaimed Oberton String Octet and performs alongside leading musicians at the Mozarthaus in Vienna. He has recordings with the Simón Bolívar String Quartet and the string trio dedicated to Gideon Klein, formed with university colleagues. In 2026, he took the first cello desk of the Camerata Strumentale Siciliana and regularly collaborates as guest first cello with the Orchestra da Camera di Pordenone.

  • Simón Bolívar String Quartet

With Tocuyito Trío (Dalina Ugarte on violin, Joseph Ávila on piano), founded in Vienna in 2023, violin, cello and piano echo the pulse of the cuatro, the bandola and the maracas. A Jeunesse Featured Ensemble for the 2023–2024 season, the trio premiered the staged concert Cambur Pin-Tón at the Wiener Konzerthaus, a co-production with Oorkaan directed by Caecilia Thunnissen and nominated for the YAMawards. With Ilumina Music, he brings Latin American repertoire to Europe's leading stages.

He began teaching at 15 and never stopped. In Catania, Sicily, he spent a year as principal cello teacher at a non-profit foundation that brings free music education to the city's most vulnerable areas, following the principles of José Antonio Abreu's Sistema. He also taught violin, viola, double bass and solfège, conducted the orchestra, and developed his own method for students with motor and visual disabilities.

Teaching and Social Outreach

Music as access, not privilege.

Santo learned in a home where music was the foundation, and has taught since he was 15. In Catania, he gave that back to a city where the instrument rarely reaches: a full year leading the cello class at a non-profit foundation, with free music education as an engine of human transformation, the principles of José Antonio Abreu's Sistema applied in Sicily.

The work included students with motor and visual disabilities. For them, he developed his own method linking technical learning to each student's emotional growth, because tuning a string and building confidence happen, in practice, at the same time.
